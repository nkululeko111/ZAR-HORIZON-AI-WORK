# Business Partner Use-Case Prioritisation

## 1. Purpose

This document prioritises the AI Business Partner use cases using a practical lens: business value, frequency, execution complexity, data requirements, integration dependency, and risk. The goal is to identify where the product can create fastest and most credible value without pretending that all use cases are equally ready for launch.

This is a prioritisation framework, not an approved product roadmap.

## 3. Priority categories

### Foundational capabilities

These should be considered the earliest value drivers because they are directly useful, observable, and repeatable.

- target customer identification
- supplier discovery and comparison
- cash-flow monitoring
- expense and profitability analysis
- sales lead follow-up
- quotation preparation
- inventory monitoring
- daily task coordination
- performance analysis and trend detection
- business document generation

### High-value capabilities

These are strong value levers but may require more customer validation and deeper workflow definition.

- competitor analysis
- customer record and pipeline management
- marketing strategy support
- growth planning and expansion support
- automated workflow execution
- financial forecasting
- external communication drafting

### Later-stage capabilities

These are valid product directions but should not define the initial MVP without more evidence.

- business idea evaluation
- partnership and supplier opportunity research

### Research required

These use cases need customer validation before being treated as core product claims.

- any highly automated business action involving external financial commitments,
- advanced expansion strategy support,
- broad AI-led business “advisory” use cases that are not clearly tied to action and outcome,
- use cases requiring sensitive customer or financial data without a well-defined permission model.

## 4. Recommended initial priority set

For an initial MVP or early product concept, the strongest starting set is:

1. customer and market identification,
2. supplier discovery and comparison,
3. sales lead follow-up,
4. quotation preparation,
5. cash-flow and profitability analysis,
6. inventory oversight,
7. task coordination and operational follow-up,
8. performance and trend reporting,
9. document generation and reporting.

This set is strongest because it is:

- immediately understandable,
- repeated in many businesses,
- tied to business value,
- likely to be visible to the user,
- manageable without broad unvalidated assumptions.

## 5. Customer validation needs

The following use cases require direct customer validation before assuming they are core to the first release:

- idea evaluation and startup planning,
- marketing strategy generation,
- growth planning and expansion support,
- automation of external communications,
- any use case involving communication or price commitments,
- financial forecasting and forecasting-driven decision support,
- any workflow that touches regulated or sensitive business decisions.

## 6. Priority conclusion

The most credible initial product wedge is not “AI for business generally.” It is a practical operating assistant for a business owner who needs help with recurring financial, operational, and sales tasks, while keeping human approval and evidence requirements in place.

That approach remains aligned with the founder-approved product definition and the need to build a product people can trust and pay for.
