# AI Business Partner Business Lifecycle Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how the AI Business Partner supports a customer through the business lifecycle. It is intentionally stage-based so the product can work for a founder at idea stage, an owner of a small established business, or a growing SME.

The framework is not a generic lifecycle model; it reflects the approved product vision for a digital business employee that supports real actions and operational decision-making.

## 2. Lifecycle stages

### Stage 1: Starting a business

#### Customer need

The customer wants to validate an idea, understand the market, and decide whether the business is viable.

#### What the agent does

- evaluates the business concept,
- researches market demand,
- identifies target customers,
- analyses likely competitors,
- estimates startup costs,
- develops a business plan,
- builds baseline financial projections,
- identifies legal and compliance considerations relevant to the business.

#### Typical outputs

- business opportunity summary,
- target customer definition,
- startup cost model,
- financial projection ranges,
- compliance and setup checklist.

#### Typical approvals

- user chooses whether to continue.
- regulatory or legal advice remains outside the product’s responsibility.

### Stage 2: Validating a business idea

#### Customer need

The customer needs evidence before investing time or money into a concept.

#### What the agent does

- compares customer pain points and buying behaviour,
- researches the competitive landscape,
- develops realistic customer segments,
- validates likely demand using external research and user-supplied information,
- estimates costs, pricing, and margin assumptions,
- highlights high-risk assumptions.

#### Typical outputs

- market assessment,
- risk and opportunity summary,
- customer segment profiles,
- pricing and margin hypotheses.

### Stage 3: Establishing and setting up operations

#### Customer need

The customer must create operational structure quickly and with clarity.

#### What the agent does

- creates setup checklists,
- helps define supplier requirements,
- identifies operational tasks,
- organises early workflows,
- prepares templates for quotes, invoices, customer communications, and business documents,
- supports forecasting and setup decisions based on business inputs.

#### Typical outputs

- operating plan,
- vendor or supplier shortlist,
- task checklist,
- templates and starter documents.

### Stage 4: Acquiring customers

#### Customer need

The customer needs to attract demand and convert interest into paying customers.

#### What the agent does

- researches target audiences,
- analyses competitor messaging,
- identifies likely channels and segments,
- prepares sales proposals and quotations,
- helps draft follow-up communication,
- tracks lead information,
- identifies prospects that should be followed up.

#### Typical outputs

- customer target list,
- lead notes,
- quotation set,
- follow-up tasks,
- sales pipeline summary.

### Stage 5: Managing daily business activities

#### Customer need

The customer must keep the business running while managing customer demand, suppliers, admin tasks, and operational visibility.

#### What the agent does

- tracks recurring tasks,
- organises business documents,
- monitors sales performance,
- identifies operational bottlenecks,
- follows up on customer or supplier actions,
- keeps reminders and tasks current,
- creates performance summaries for daily or weekly review.

#### Typical outputs

- operations dashboard,
- task tracker,
- scheduled reminders,
- operational summary reports.

### Stage 6: Managing finances and resources

#### Customer need

The customer needs visibility into profitability, expenses, and cash flow to make informed decisions.

#### What the agent does

- tracks revenue and expense patterns,
- monitors cash-flow trends,
- identifies unusual financial movement,
- calculates margin and break-even assumptions,
- compares actual performance against targets,
- prepares monthly business summaries.

#### Typical outputs

- financial health report,
- cash-flow timeline,
- margin and break-even analysis,
- exception alerts and variance notes.

#### Important boundary

The product does not act as a bank, auditor, accountant, or regulated adviser. It may analyse financial information, but it must clearly separate verified data, user-supplied figures, calculations, assumptions, and estimates.

### Stage 7: Improving performance

#### Customer need

The customer needs to identify underperformance and what needs to change.

#### What the agent does

- diagnoses operational inefficiencies,
- identifies underperforming sales or campaigns,
- compares performance over time,
- detects trends and unusual movement,
- recommends specific actions,
- monitors whether actions produce measurable improvement.

#### Typical outputs

- performance review report,
- improvement recommendations,
- action plan,
- outcome tracking notes.

### Stage 8: Expanding and growing the business

#### Customer need

The customer needs to scale without losing control or becoming operationally fragmented.

#### What the agent does

- researches markets and opportunities,
- compares expansion options,
- analyses cost and benefit trade-offs,
- evaluates supplier or partnership options,
- supports pricing and growth planning,
- monitors cash, pipeline, and operational performance,
- prepares decision-support reports for scale-up actions.

#### Typical outputs

- growth strategy summary,
- expansion options analysis,
- performance comparison roll-up,
- operational decision brief.

## 3. Lifecycle principle

The AI Business Partner is useful across all maturity levels because the same operational logic can be applied in different ways:

- new business: idea validation, startup planning, setup checklists, financial planning
- small business: daily operations, supplier and customer management, workflow support, performance analysis
- growing business: sales intelligence, financial performance review, operational optimisation, growth planning

A single agent can support the customer at different stages, but it must remain grounded in the customer’s actual business information and evidence.

## 4. How the customer experiences the product

Customers should experience the AI Business Partner as a practical operating partner that helps them:

- understand the business,
- see what is happening,
- know what to do next,
- act where authorised,
- verify the result,
- explain the reasoning and evidence.

## 5. Lifecycle dependencies

The lifecycle is not fully linear. A business can move back and forth between improvement, operations, and growth. The product should support iterative improvement rather than forcing a rigid path.

Examples:

- a business may need to revisit supplier strategy after cost growth,
- a service business may need to re-evaluate customer acquisition while improving follow-up discipline,
- a growing SME may need to rebalance cash-flow forecasting as it expands.

## 6. Key design implication

The agent must be designed around business operations, not merely business advice. The value lies in:

- understanding the customer’s context,
- pulling in the right information,
- reasoning through business constraints,
- performing the approved action,
- verifying the result,
- reporting with evidence.
