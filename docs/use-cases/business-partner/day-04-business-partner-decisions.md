# Day 4 Business Partner Decisions

## 1. Purpose

This document records the decisions that shape the Day 4 use-case work for the AI Business Partner. It distinguishes between decisions already supported by the founder-approved foundation and decisions that still require customer validation or founder approval.

## 2. Decisions already established by the approved product definition

The following are already established as product-direction decisions:

- The AI Business Partner is an execution-oriented business agent, not a generic chatbot.
- It must support the full business lifecycle from idea validation to growth.
- It must research, reason, use tools, execute approved tasks, verify outcomes, and report results.
- It must distinguish verified information from estimates, assumptions, and recommendations.
- It must operate within a shared platform but must not inherit offensive security authority or cross into security execution.
- It must support real business work, not just explanation or conversation.

## 3. Core Business Partner decisions established by Day 4 analysis

These decisions are consistent with the product vision and the research done so far:

1. The Business Partner should be organised around operational stages, not only feature categories.
2. The strongest early value is in recurring business workflows such as lead follow-up, quotation preparation, supplier comparison, financial summaries, and operational task management.
3. The product should support owners and teams working across different levels of business maturity.
4. The product should help convert information into action, not just provide advice.
5. The product must explicitly separate verified data from assumptions and calculations.
6. The product must maintain a clear approval model for commercial communication and sensitive financial actions.

## 4. Use cases that are strongest for early product positioning

These use cases are strongest for an initial product focus:

- sales lead follow-up,
- supplier discovery and comparison,
- quotation preparation,
- financial summary and cash-flow monitoring,
- profitability analysis,
- inventory and stock oversight,
- recurring task and reminder coordination,
- business document generation,
- performance analysis and trend reporting.

These are the most credible initial value drivers because they are frequently experienced, operationally visible, and likely to produce tangible business outcomes.

## 5. Use cases requiring customer validation before prioritisation

These should not be treated as core or final product claims without direct customer validation:

- startup idea evaluation,
- advanced market opportunity research,
- growth planning and expansion support,
- broader marketing strategy automation,
- broad “general business advisor” use cases,
- financial forecasting used for real commercial decisions,
- highly automated external communication workflows,
- any use case that depends on sensitive customer or employee data.

## 6. Decisions requiring founder approval

The following should be presented to the founder for approval before they are treated as formal product commitments:

1. Which initial use-case set becomes the MVP wedge.
2. Whether the product should prioritise small-business operations before broader startup or growth planning.
3. Whether supplier and financial analysis should be deeply automated in early product versions.
4. The acceptable level of human review required for external communications and quote generation.
5. Whether the product is positioned primarily for owner-managed small businesses or also for broader SME scalability.
6. The level of automation allowed for tasks that touch financial or commercial commitments.

## 7. Key implementation implications

The Day 4 analysis supports the following design implications:

- the product should support frequent, repeated business tasks that are visible to the user,
- it should remain evidence-based and transparent about assumptions,
- it should be designed around a practical operating workflow and not only reporting,
- it should use approval checkpoints where external communications or financial outcomes are involved,
- it should stay grounded in business execution rather than general AI conversation.

## 8. Final decision summary

The AI Business Partner should be built around practical business execution, clear evidence, and controlled automation. The strongest near-term direction is a system that helps business owners manage operations, finances, customer follow-up, and supplier-related decisions while keeping human review in place for high-risk or externally sensitive actions.

This approach remains consistent with the founder-approved product definition and with the current business research findings.
