# Business Agent Execution Workflows

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the standard execution workflow of the AI Business Partner. Each workflow is designed to move the agent from a user objective to a validated result while respecting the product’s evidence, permission, and approval requirements.

## 2. Standard workflow pattern

Every major capability follows the same pattern:

1. Understand the user’s objective.
2. Gather the necessary business information.
3. Identify missing information.
4. Research or retrieve relevant information.
5. Analyse the situation.
6. Develop an execution plan.
7. Request approval when required.
8. Execute via authorised tools.
9. Verify the outcome.
10. Report what happened and what remains outstanding.

## 3. Workflow A: Market validation workflow

### Scenario

A founder wants to start a food business and needs to determine whether demand exists in a particular area.

### Workflow

1. Understand the objective: assess whether there is enough demand and a realistic market fit.
2. Gather information: target area, offering, customer profile, pricing assumptions, competitor landscape.
3. Identify missing information: local demand indicators, customer spending patterns, competitor offerings.
4. Research: gather public market signals, local business information, competitor details, and the user’s own data.
5. Analyse: compare market fit, likely customer segments, price sensitivity, and startup constraint.
6. Develop execution plan: produce a market opportunity brief and identify next actions.
7. Request approval: if the user intends to launch or spend money, they approve the plan before execution.
8. Execute: prepare a market opportunity summary or a startup plan.
9. Verify: check that the analysis is based on usable evidence and explicitly label assumptions.
10. Report: provide a summary of findings, evidence quality, conclusions, and areas needing confirmation.

## 4. Workflow B: Supplier comparison workflow

### Scenario

A retail or manufacturing business needs to source packaging or raw materials.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: compare suppliers and identify the most suitable option.
2. Gather information: product specifications, budget, quantity, required quality, delivery requirements, region.
3. Identify missing information: exact product requirements, acceptable lead times, supplier terms.
4. Research: gather supplier options, prices, delivery terms, and quality notes.
5. Analyse: compare price, quality risk, lead time, reliability, and total cost.
6. Develop execution plan: shortlist suppliers and prepare comparison criteria.
7. Request approval: approval is needed before sending requests for quotation or final supplier decisions.
8. Execute: draft RFQ, compare quotations, create supplier comparison sheet.
9. Verify: ensure comparison uses consistent assumptions and verified figures.
10. Report: summarise the best option and why, including any assumptions or missing details.

## 5. Workflow C: Quotation preparation workflow

### Scenario

A service business needs to send a quotation for a new customer.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: prepare a customer-facing quotation.
2. Gather information: scope, costs, pricing assumptions, suitable margins, business terms.
3. Identify missing information: exact deliverables, time assumptions, cost data, labour assumptions.
4. Research: check pricing, customer context, and market norms if needed.
5. Analyse: calculate costs, margin, and commercial fit.
6. Develop execution plan: create a quote structure with assumptions and terms.
7. Request approval: explicit approval before sending externally.
8. Execute: draft quotation and proposal document.
9. Verify: confirm product/service details, calculations, and any pricing assumptions.
10. Report: provide the final quotation and record the assumptions and approval status.

## 6. Workflow D: Sales follow-up workflow

### Scenario

A small business wants to follow up with leads that were not converted.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: identify which leads need action and prepare follow-up messaging.
2. Gather information: lead list, status, last interaction, sales notes, customer profile, buying history.
3. Identify missing information: whether there is a prior relationship, required offer, or renewal window.
4. Research: review customer context, recent activity, and likely needs.
5. Analyse: identify which leads are warm, stale, or high-priority.
6. Develop execution plan: group leads, determine follow-up order, and choose messaging approach.
7. Request approval: email or customer communication may require user approval before sending.
8. Execute: update CRM notes, create tasks, draft follow-up messages.
9. Verify: confirm the CRM record is updated and the tasks are assigned correctly.
10. Report: summarise which leads were updated, why they were chosen, and what remains pending.

## 7. Workflow E: Financial review workflow

### Scenario

A business owner wants to understand why profit appears lower than expected.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: investigate the financial issue and identify the likely drivers.
2. Gather information: revenue records, expenses, invoices, costs, stock movement, payroll, and context from the user.
3. Identify missing information: incomplete categories, missing data periods, untracked costs, assumptions.
4. Research: review data sources and identify the relevant time period.
5. Analyse: compare revenue, costs, margins, and operating ratios.
6. Develop execution plan: create a financial summary and identify likely problem drivers.
7. Request approval: any proposed action with financial consequence or external communication may require approval.
8. Execute: prepare a variance summary, cash-flow view, and recommendations.
9. Verify: ensure figures are correctly sourced and assumptions are explicitly labelled.
10. Report: provide a clear explanation of driver changes, the confidence level, and the remaining unknowns.

## 8. Workflow F: Performance improvement workflow

### Scenario

A business is struggling with inconsistent sales performance or weak marketing results.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: identify the likely cause of underperformance and the right intervention.
2. Gather information: sales metrics, campaign data, inventory, costs, customer trends, and operational notes.
3. Identify missing information: conversion performance, campaign spend, customer feedback, or attribution data.
4. Research: compare performance by campaign, channel, product, and time period.
5. Analyse: identify trends, issues, and likely causes.
6. Develop execution plan: recommend improvements with measurable expected outcomes.
7. Request approval: approval for campaign changes, customer communications, or spend decisions.
8. Execute: update campaigns, create tasks, prepare improved content, or refine follow-up.
9. Verify: measure whether the changed workflow or campaign yields the expected outcome.
10. Report: summarise findings, expected improvements, and actual results.

## 9. Workflow G: Inventory and stock oversight workflow

### Scenario

A retailer or wholesaler wants to improve stock control and reduce losses or shortages.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: reduce stock issues and improve inventory visibility.
2. Gather information: inventory levels, sales velocity, stock movement, supplier lead time, product categories.
3. Identify missing information: stock variance, reorder points, seasonal demand, lead-time reliability.
4. Research: compare stock performance versus sales, historical patterns, and supplier behaviour.
5. Analyse: identify inventory risks and opportunities.
6. Develop execution plan: prepare reorder recommendations and exception alerts.
7. Request approval: changes to stock purchase decisions or supplier choices typically require approval.
8. Execute: update inventory records, prepare reorder recommendations, schedule actions.
9. Verify: confirm the updated stock records and proposed actions are accurate.
10. Report: share stock concerns, recommended actions, and any assumptions.

## 10. Workflow H: Operational task coordination workflow

### Scenario

A small business wants to organise recurring admin tasks and keep team activity on schedule.

### Workflow

1. Understand objective: create a structured operational task system.
2. Gather information: recurring tasks, owners, deadlines, dependencies, and workflow issues.
3. Identify missing information: responsible owner, required frequency, urgency, or time estimates.
4. Research: review the task history if available.
5. Analyse: determine the highest-risk and highest-value tasks.
6. Develop execution plan: create task ownership and reminder plan.
7. Request approval: approval may be required for external actions or task allocations.
8. Execute: create reminders, tasks, and reporting templates.
9. Verify: confirm the scheduled tasks or process actions are correctly assigned.
10. Report: summarise tasks and outstanding actions.

## 11. Execution requirements and evidence standards

For each workflow, the system must show:

- what data was used,
- why it was needed,
- what the agent concluded,
- what it executed,
- how it verified success,
- what assumptions remain,
- what approval or follow-up is still required.

## 12. Approval model for workflow execution

### Low-risk actions

Examples:

- summarising a report,
- drafting customer communication for review,
- preparing a task list,
- building a spreadsheet comparison for user review,
- flagging a business issue for attention.

Approval: usually user review or explicit command.

### Medium-risk actions

Examples:

- sending a customer quote,
- updating CRM records,
- updating stock or supplier information,
- creating external communications,
- generating invoices or client-facing documents.

Approval: explicit human approval is expected before sending or modifying external data.

### High-risk actions

Examples:

- committing to a supplier or contract,
- making a financial decision based on assumptions,
- automatically sending customer commitments,
- processing payments or payment instructions,
- passing sensitive business data to external systems without review.

Approval: must require explicit human approval and should be subject to audit logging and traceability.

## 13. Final principle

The AI Business Partner is successful when it acts as a disciplined business operator, not a generic advisor. It must convert information to practical action, verify outcomes, and communicate evidence clearly. The fact that it uses AI does not reduce the requirement for accuracy, traceability, and approval when actions affect customers, finances, suppliers, or business commitments.
