# AI Business Partner Use-Case Catalogue

## 1. Purpose

This catalogue defines the operational use cases for the AI Business Partner as an execution-oriented business agent. It is grounded in the founder-approved product definition: the product helps people start, operate, manage, and grow a business; it must research, reason, use tools, execute approved tasks, verify outcomes, and report clearly.

This is not a generic chatbot catalogue. Every use case is written as a business capability with an operational workflow, expected outputs, required inputs, approval path, and evidence of completion.

## 2. Product purpose and execution model

The AI Business Partner supports a business lifecycle that moves through:

- idea validation and startup preparation,
- setup and operational foundation,
- customer acquisition and sales execution,
- operational management,
- financial oversight,
- growth planning,
- continuous improvement and reporting.

Across all stages, it operates under the product philosophy:

RESEARCH → REASON → USE TOOLS → EXECUTE → VERIFY → REPORT.

It must distinguish between:

- verified business data,
- external research,
- calculations,
- estimates,
- assumptions,
- recommendations.

It must not present one category as another. It must clearly label what is known, estimated, or advised.

## 4. Detailed use-case definitions

### 4.1 Business idea and startup intelligence

#### Use case: Evaluate a business idea

- Objective: assess whether a business idea has sufficient customer need, commercial logic, and viability.
- Inputs: business concept, target market, current evidence, startup assumptions, pricing ideas.
- Outputs: opportunity summary, demand signals, risks, and next-step actions.
- Approval: typically user-driven; may require review before acting on a launch decision.
- Verification: compare findings to user-supplied facts and public market evidence.

#### Use case: Research market demand

- Objective: determine whether there is evidence of market interest and demand.
- Inputs: sector, geography, customer segment, product or service concept.
- Outputs: market opportunity summary with evidence notes.
- Verification: confirm evidence source, date, and relevance.

#### Use case: Identify target customers

- Objective: define segments with realistic buying intent and need.
- Outputs: customer segments, needs, buying triggers, and value proposition fit.

#### Use case: Estimate startup costs

- Objective: construct a practical startup cost model.
- Inputs: capital items, monthly costs, supplier quotations, assumptions.
- Outputs: startup cost estimate, cost categories, and assumptions.
- Verification: calculations must be visible and traceable.

### 4.2 Market and customer intelligence

#### Use case: Research industries and markets

- Objective: provide structured market context for a business owner.
- Outputs: market overview, competitive landscape, likely demands, risk factors.

#### Use case: Identify potential customers

- Objective: find commercial prospects using user-supplied constraints or local research.
- Outputs: prospect list, customer fit summary, and notes on likely buying intent.
- Approval: external outreach, contact actions, or direct communications may require approval.

### 4.3 Supplier and procurement intelligence

#### Use case: Discover suppliers and compare pricing

- Objective: identify likely suppliers and compare costs or terms.
- Inputs: product or service requirements, geography, volume, quality constraints.
- Outputs: supplier list, comparison sheet, and suitability notes.
- Verification: inspect quotes and compare consistent units and assumptions.

### 4.4 Financial intelligence

#### Use case: Monitor cash flow and profitability

- Objective: help the user understand how money moves through the business.
- Inputs: invoices, expenses, revenue, payroll, stock movement, bank data if connected.
- Outputs: cash-flow summary, variances, trends, and risk signals.
- Important boundary: the agent is not a bank, accountant, auditor, or regulated financial adviser.

### 4.5 Sales and customer management

#### Use case: Prepare a quotation

- Objective: convert business information into a customer-facing quote.
- Inputs: product or service details, pricing assumptions, supplier cost, margin logic.
- Outputs: customer quotation, assumptions, and pricing logic.
- Approval: may require explicit confirmation before sending externally.

### 4.6 Marketing and business development

#### Use case: Analyse campaign performance

- Objective: identify which marketing efforts are delivering value.
- Inputs: campaign data, leads, conversion metrics, spend data.
- Outputs: performance summary, recommended improvements, and test ideas.

### 4.7 Operations and administration

#### Use case: Manage recurring operational workflows

- Objective: keep operational tasks organised and on schedule.
- Inputs: tasks, deadlines, reminders, owners, workflows.
- Outputs: tasks, schedules, reminders, and status notes.

### 4.8 Business performance and decision intelligence

#### Use case: Explain why a metric changed

- Objective: connect a change in a business metric to specific drivers.
- Inputs: revenue, expenses, volume, margin, stock, sales, seasonal patterns.
- Outputs: driver analysis with evidence and considerations.
- Verification: separate verified numbers from assumptions and estimates.

### 4.9 Agent execution and tool usage

#### Use case: Update an approved CRM record

- Objective: record information about a contact or lead.
- Inputs: CRM access, user approval, lead-specific data.
- Outputs: updated record and audit trail.
- Approval: explicit permission required.
- Verification: confirmation of saved record and field-level results.

## 5. Execution requirements by use case

Every use case should document:

- required information,
- source of truth,
- tools or integrations potentially required,
- read-only vs modification actions,
- approval requirements,
- verification method,
- output evidence.

Examples:

- Read-only research task: market scan → requires public sources and user context; verification by source quality and relevance.
- Write action: update CRM → requires authorised integration and user permission; verification by saved record and notification.
- Financial estimate: forecast break-even → requires assumptions and calculation notes; verification by formula transparency and clear labels of assumptions.

## 6. Output standards for all use cases

Each use case should produce one or more of the following:

- analysis report,
- comparison sheet,
- operational task list,
- draft document,
- email or outgoing communication,
- status update,
- action log,
- evidence-backed recommendation,
- variance explanation,
- summary with close-out status.

The agent must always report:

- what it did,
- what information it used,
- what it concludes,
- what assumptions remain,
- what approval is still required,
- what remains outstanding.

## 7. Use-case quality gates

A Business Partner use case should be considered valid only if it satisfies all of the following:

- it supports a real business problem,
- it is relevant to starting, operating, or growing a business,
- it can be performed with tools or approved workflow actions,
- it is not merely advice without execution,
- it can be verified after completion,
- it produces a clear business result.

## 8. Summary

The Business Partner use-case catalogue supports both breadth and execution. The product is designed to support customers at different stages of business maturity, from idea validation through scaling. The use cases together form a practical operating model for an AI business agent that helps owners move from information to action while staying within the approved boundaries of evidence, automation, and human approval.
