# AI Hacker Commercial Delivery Models

## 1. Purpose

This document defines potential delivery models for the AI Hacker without finalising pricing or product decisions. It outlines how the product could be sold or delivered while remaining consistent with the founder-approved vision and with the customer needs identified in the market research documents.

## 2. Potential delivery models

### Model 1: Self-service security assessments

#### Description

An organisation uses the product as a guided security assessment tool for defined assets and targets.

#### Best fit

- software teams,
- early-stage startups,
- teams with basic internal technical ownership,
- businesses that want fast assessment support without a large external engagement.

#### Responsibilities

- the customer provides the target assets and scope,
- the product handles discovery, validation, reporting, and remediation guidance,
- human security review may still be necessary for high-risk findings.

#### Value proposition

- lower friction than a full consultancy engagement,
- speed and repeatability,
- useful for periodic assessment needs.

### Model 2: Subscription-based recurring assessments

#### Description

A customer pays a recurring fee for regular security assessments and reporting.

#### Best fit

- SaaS companies,
- recurring digital product businesses,
- MSPs and service providers,
- organisations with repeated release cycles or ongoing target changes.

#### Responsibilities

- tool or service is used on a regular cadence,
- periodic assessments and retesting become part of a recurring service model,
- human review remains required for high-risk findings or any disputed result.

#### Value proposition

- predictable cost,
- regular posture checks,
- stronger ongoing security coverage.

### Model 3: Project-based security testing

#### Description

A customer commissions a specific security assessment for a release, acquisition, or major platform change.

#### Best fit

- software development companies,
- digital platforms preparing a launch,
- businesses responding to a suspected weakness,
- organisations seeking a defined assessment with a clear scope.

#### Responsibilities

- the project is time-boxed and scope-defined,
- assessment outputs are delivered as a formal report,
- follow-up retesting is handled as a separate phase if needed.

#### Value proposition

- clear commercial structure,
- deterministic scope,
- useful for a release checkpoint or security review.

### Model 4: MSP or agency-assisted delivery

#### Description

An MSP or service provider uses the product as part of their client delivery workflow.

#### Best fit

- IT service providers,
- managed security service providers,
- agencies with client environments and recurring technical support relationships.

#### Responsibilities

- the MSP owns the customer relationship,
- the AI Hacker supports assessment, validation, and reporting,
- human security specialists may need to review and sign off on high-risk findings.

#### Value proposition

- recurring client service model,
- expanded service capability,
- partner-based distribution model.

### Model 5: Continuous security assessment

#### Description

The product is used to monitor an authorised environment continuously or on a recurring schedule.

#### Best fit

- organisations with dynamic cloud or web exposure,
- digital businesses with rapid change,
- customers requiring repeated posture checks.

#### Responsibilities

- regular checks,
- drift detection,
- repeated testing and reporting,
- human oversight for high-risk findings.

#### Value proposition

- improved speed of detection,
- useful for businesses with real-time security change rates.

### Model 6: Remediation and retesting packages

#### Description

The customer pays for a verification loop after a finding is identified and fixed.

#### Best fit

- any customer with serious findings,
- teams that want proof that fixes actually work,
- organisations that need repeated remediations and closures.

#### Responsibilities

- the agent validates whether the fix has worked,
- a human reviewer may be needed for a final sign-off on critical findings,
- final status is updated only after verification.

#### Value proposition

- higher trust in remediation,
- less risk of false closure,
- better operational discipline.

## 3. Customer segments most likely to match each model

| Model | Most likely customer segment |
| --- | --- |
| Self-service assessments | software startup, small SaaS, digital business |
| Subscription model | recurring digital product teams, SaaS, MSPs |
| Project-based security testing | software companies, product teams facing a release or issue |
| MSP-assisted delivery | IT service providers and MSPs |
| Continuous assessment | businesses with cloud and active digital exposure |
| Remediation and retesting | any customer with genuine findings and verification needs |

## 4. Human security responsibilities

Even in a product-delivery model, the AI Hacker should not be treated as a complete replacement for specialist human security review. Some responsibilities likely require professional oversight:

- high-risk intrusive validation,
- privilege-boundary testing,
- enterprise-level risk interpretation,
- final sign-off on findings with major business impact,
- legal or contractual review for sensitive testing engagements.

## 5. Product-delivery caution

This document is not an approval of pricing or a final product model. It is a strategic outline only. Final commercial decisions should be based on customer validation and founder review rather than assumptions about what buyers want.

## 6. Summary

The AI Hacker can be delivered through multiple models, but the most credible models are those that align with real customer needs, approved testing scope, and clear evidence requirements. The strongest early commercial patterns are likely to be subscription or project-based assessment models with retesting, rather than broad security automation with no human and process controls.
