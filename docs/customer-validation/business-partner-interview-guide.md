# AI Business Partner Interview Guide

## 1. Purpose

This conversation is a customer-discovery interview, not a sales pitch. The goal is to understand the real operational problems facing a business and whether those problems are serious and frequent enough to justify paying for an AI agent that executes practical business work.

Target duration: 20–30 minutes.

## 2. Interview principles

- Start with the customer’s business context.
- Ask open-ended questions.
- Do not introduce the product too early.
- Do not suggest that a solution already exists.
- Focus on problems, workflows, costs, and current coping mechanisms.
- Listen for urgency, financial pain, and manual effort.

## 3. Interview flow

### Section A: Business background

1. Can you tell me a bit about your business and how it operates today?
2. What are the main services or products you sell?
3. How do customers usually find you and buy from you?
4. What does a typical week look like for you and the team?
5. What parts of the business feel most time-consuming or stressful?

### Section B: Operational pain and recurring problems

6. What are the biggest recurring problems in the business right now?
7. Which problems happen weekly or monthly and keep coming back?
8. Which problems create the most stress or disruption?
9. Are there tasks that are repeated manually every week?
10. Where do things slow down or get missed?

### Section C: Cash flow and financial visibility

11. How do you currently know whether the business is doing well or under pressure?
12. How do you track revenue, expenses, and cash flow?
13. Where do you usually feel blind spots in the numbers?
14. What happens when you do not have clear visibility into cash flow or profitability?
15. How often do financial numbers get updated or reviewed?

### Section D: Inventory, suppliers, and sales operations

16. How do you manage stock, inventory, or supplier information today?
17. What causes stock issues or supplier problems in your business?
18. How do you find new customers or leads today?
19. How do you handle follow-up after a lead or sales conversation?
20. Where do opportunities get lost or delayed?

### Section E: Business admin and workflow execution

21. Which admin tasks take the most time each week?
22. What parts of invoicing, quote preparation, or customer communication are painful?
23. Are there tasks you wish someone could automatically handle or follow up on?
24. Which business processes feel repetitive and easy to miss?
25. If you could remove some of the admin burden, where would it matter most?

### Section F: Current tools and spend

26. What software or tools do you currently use for sales, accounting, customer management, or operations?
27. What do you pay for those tools or services each month?
28. How much time is spent on manual admin or chasing tasks?
29. What is not working well with your current setup?
30. Do you outsource any part of this work today?

### Section G: Pain cost and urgency

31. When these problems happen, what is the impact on revenue, time, or stress?
32. What is the cost of a missed lead, an unpaid invoice, a stock issue, or delayed follow-up?
33. How urgently do you need this improved?
34. What would make this problem worth solving right now?
35. If we could help with this operationally, what would success look like?

### Section H: AI and trust

36. Have you used AI tools before? If yes, what for?
37. What kinds of work would you trust an AI system to do for your business?
38. What would make you uncomfortable about letting an AI system handle business tasks?
39. How much human review would you want before a task is executed?
40. What level of control would you want over a system that helps run parts of the business?

### Section I: Buying decision and budget

41. Who usually decides on software or service purchases in the business?
42. What would need to be true before you would consider paying for a solution like this?
43. Would you prefer a subscription, a project-based service, or something else?
44. Is there a budget for tools that improve operations and reduce admin effort?
45. What would be the strongest proof that a solution is worth buying?

## 4. Closing

46. Is there anything else you would want a system like this to handle?
47. If we were to explore this further, would it be reasonable to follow up with a second conversation?

## 5. What to listen for

Strong signals include:

- repeated daily or weekly pain,
- clear time wasted,
- existing spending on software or admin help,
- frustration with current tools,
- evidence of lost revenue or missed opportunities,
- willingness to discuss budget or trade-offs,
- concern around execution quality and visibility.

Weak signals include:

- vague interest in AI,
- no current problem,
- no existing workaround,
- no spending on tools or services,
- no urgency or clear improvement target.

## 6. Red flags

Avoid asking interviewees to reveal:

- confidential customer data,
- pricing strategy,
- internal financial detail beyond what is necessary,
- personal or sensitive information that is not relevant to the problem.

## 7. Interview best practice

- Keep the tone conversational and respectful.
- Do not rush into product language.
- Do not ask for permission to “sell” them after the interview.
- Capture direct quotes where possible.
- Document pain, spend, and bottlenecks accurately.
- Record whether the person is a decision-maker, influencer, or just a contact.
