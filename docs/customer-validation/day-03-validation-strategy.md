# Day 3 Validation Strategy

## 1. Purpose

This programme is designed to move ZAR HORIZON AI @WORK from assumptions to evidence. The goal is not to sell an unfinished product. The goal is to test whether the problems identified in Day 1 and Day 2 are real, frequent, costly, and important enough that a customer would pay for a system that actually executes the work.

This is a founder-led customer discovery effort for two independent products:

- AI Business Partner
- AI Hacker

## 2. Validation principles

1. Start with the customer problem, not the product.
2. Talk to people who have the problem, not just people who are enthusiastic about AI.
3. Ask open-ended questions and avoid leading the customer toward a desired answer.
4. Distinguish between interest, pain, and actual buying intent.
5. Validate the cost of the problem and whether the customer already spends money to solve it.
6. Do not ask for confidential security details or sensitive business data.
7. Keep interviews focused and respectful of time.
8. Record objective evidence, direct quotes, and observed buying signals.

## 3. Validated customer segments

### AI Business Partner target segments

1. Micro and informal businesses
2. Established small businesses
3. Growing SMEs

Primary profile:

- owner-managed service or retail businesses,
- recurring revenue,
- operational bottlenecks,
- limited internal admin support,
- need for better visibility and execution support.

### AI Hacker target segments

1. IT service providers and MSPs
2. Software development companies
3. Startups and SaaS companies

Primary profile:

- customer-facing digital products,
- limited internal security support,
- regular release cycles or product growth,
- need for practical security assessment,
- concern about cost and speed of traditional testing.

## 4. Validation objective by product

### AI Business Partner

The key question is not whether a business wants “AI.” The key question is whether the business has recurring operational problems that are painful enough to justify paying for AI-powered execution support.

Critical problem areas to validate:

- cash-flow visibility,
- stock and inventory management,
- supplier discovery and management,
- customer acquisition,
- lead follow-up,
- quotation and invoice administration,
- manual business processes,
- financial reporting,
- business performance monitoring.

### AI Hacker

The key question is whether customers have security testing bottlenecks that are painful enough to justify a product that can assess, validate, prioritise, and support remediation.

Critical problem areas to validate:

- vulnerability testing bottlenecks,
- cost of traditional penetration testing,
- security testing before release,
- API security,
- website security,
- cloud infrastructure security,
- lack of internal security specialists,
- vulnerability validation,
- reporting and retesting.

## 5. Validation funnel

### Stage 1: screening and recruitment

Recruit a broad but relevant pool of prospects and filter them for fit.

Focus on:

- local business owners,
- founders,
- operations leads,
- software founders,
- CTOs,
- developers,
- IT managers,
- MSP owners,
- security professionals.

### Stage 2: interviews

Complete:

- 10 AI Business Partner interviews
- 10 AI Hacker interviews

The first interviews are discovery interviews. They are not proof. They are evidence-generation conversations.

### Stage 3: synthesis

After each interview, assess:

- pain level,
- frequency,
- cost of problem,
- spending already in place,
- decision-maker status,
- urgency,
- practicality of a solution,
- willingness to continue the conversation.

### Stage 4: decision point

Use the evidence to decide whether to:

- continue with the segment,
- narrow the use case,
- adjust the customer profile,
- revisit the pricing model,
- conduct more research before moving into MVP definition.

These decisions must be presented to the founder for approval before any product strategy changes are made.

## 6. Recruitment strategy for South Africa

### Recommended channels

#### For AI Business Partner

- local business associations and chambers,
- township and suburban business communities,
- SMB WhatsApp and local business groups,
- LinkedIn outreach to founder-led service and retail businesses,
- referrals from accountants, business consultants, and local professional networks,
- startup and SME incubator contacts,
- local commerce and trade groups.

#### For AI Hacker

- software development communities,
- startup founder networks,
- CTO and engineering communities,
- MSP and IT services networks,
- LinkedIn outreach to founders, CTOs, and security-conscious teams,
- developer and product communities,
- digital agencies and software partners,
- referrals from trusted technical contacts.

### Recruitment guardrails

- Do not recruit only from friends, warm leads, or people who already support the idea.
- Avoid only interviewing people with obvious enthusiasm for AI.
- Use multiple outreach channels to access real buyers and decision-makers.
- Screen for actual business or technical relevance before scheduling.

## 7. Interview plan and target volume

Target: 10 interviews per product.

Recommended mix:

### AI Business Partner

- 4 interviews with small service or retail businesses
- 3 interviews with established small businesses
- 3 interviews with growing SMEs

### AI Hacker

- 4 interviews with software companies or SaaS teams
- 3 interviews with technical founders or CTOs
- 3 interviews with MSP or IT provider decision-makers

This mix keeps the sample practical while still covering the primary segments identified in Day 2.

## 8. Interview quality bar

An interview counts as valid only when it provides usable evidence about:

- actual business or technical pain,
- current workaround or tooling,
- frequency and severity of the problem,
- whether the issue is solved manually or through outsourced help,
- whether there is spending or budget pressure behind it,
- whether the customer is in a position to make or influence a purchase decision.

Interest alone is not enough.

## 9. Evidence required before MVP definition

Before moving into MVP definition, the team needs evidence that:

- the problem is real,
- it happens frequently,
- it is costly or risky,
- current solutions are inadequate,
- the customer has some willingness to pay,
- the acquisition path is practical,
- the product can be framed around an execution outcome, not just “AI features.”

## 10. Key considerations for founder approval

The founder should review the validation results for:

- customer pain intensity,
- evidence of budget or spending,
- segment confidence,
- fit with the approved product vision,
- repeatability of the problem,
- willingness to test or pay,
- need for product narrowing.

This is the evidence gate before product definition and MVP scoping.

## 11. Outcome of this validation stage

This stage is intended to answer a single question:

Do real people have real, recurring problems that are serious enough to pay for a system that performs work in their context?

If the answer is yes, the product can move forward with more confidence. If not, the product or target segment should be revised or narrowed with founder approval.
