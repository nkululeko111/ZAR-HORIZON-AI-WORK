# Customer Discovery Scorecard

## 1. Scorecard purpose

This scorecard is used to assess the strength of each customer interview. It should be completed immediately after the conversation and updated with follow-up evidence.

The purpose is to reduce bias and prevent a polite conversation from being mistaken for a validated commercial opportunity.

## 2. Customer interview scorecard

| Field | Example / scoring guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Interview ID | Unique interview reference |
| Date | YYYY-MM-DD |

| Segment | Business Partner or AI Hacker segment |
| Interviewee role | Founder, CEO, CTO, developer, IT manager, MSP owner etc. |

| Current solutions | Existing tools, services, or manual processes |
| Current spending | Record actual amount if disclosed |

| Problem severity | 1–5 |
| Problem frequency | 1–5 |
| Financial impact | 1–5 |
| Dissatisfaction with existing solutions | 1–5 |

## 3. Scoring guidance

### Problem severity

- 1 = minor inconvenience
- 2 = annoying but manageable
- 3 = meaningful operational or technical pain
- 4 = serious recurring issue with broad impact
- 5 = critical, urgent, or business-threatening issue

### Problem frequency

- 1 = rare
- 2 = occasional
- 3 = monthly
- 4 = weekly
- 5 = daily or near-daily

### Financial impact

- 1 = low/no clear cost
- 2 = small recurring cost
- 3 = moderate impact on efficiency or spend
- 4 = clear revenue or cost pressure
- 5 = major cost, risk, or revenue loss

### Dissatisfaction with existing solutions

- 1 = not dissatisfied
- 2 = neutral
- 3 = some dissatisfaction
- 4 = notably frustrated
- 5 = active frustration / strong need for better solution

### Urgency to solve

- 1 = no urgency
- 2 = could wait
- 3 = eventually important
- 4 = time-sensitive
- 5 = must be solved now

### Trust in AI execution

- 1 = no trust
- 2 = cautious
- 3 = mixed views
- 4 = open to some AI-assisted execution
- 5 = willing to consider AI execution with controls

## 4. Buying signal classification

Use this classification after the interview:

- General interest: “This sounds interesting.”
- Problem acknowledgement: “Yes, that is a problem.”
- Active search: “We have been looking for a better way.”
- Existing spending: “We already pay for tools or services.”
- Willingness to test: “We would be open to a pilot or trial.”
- Willingness to pay: “We could consider paying if value is clear.”
- Actual commitment: explicit budget, procurement discussion, or next-step agreement.

## 5. Interview quality rules

- A polite expression of interest is not a buying signal.
- A customer who says they have a problem is not automatically a target customer.
- A customer who already pays for a workaround is stronger evidence than a customer who only says the issue matters.
- A customer with decision-making authority and a real budget is materially more valuable than an enthusiast with no buying power.

## 6. Suggested summary format

Use this short format after each interview:

- The problem is: [one sentence]
- The pain is: [severity and frequency]
- Current solution: [tools or process]
- Spend today: [amount if disclosed]
- Most important signal: [interest / urgency / spend / decision-making]
- Recommended next step: [follow-up / no follow-up / referral / deeper validation]
