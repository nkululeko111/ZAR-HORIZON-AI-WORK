# AI Hacker Interview Guide

## 1. Purpose

This interview is designed to validate whether real technical teams face repeated security testing bottlenecks and whether they would value a controlled, evidence-backed AI security assessment product.

The conversation is for technical decision-makers and security-conscious operators, not for informal brainstorming.

Target duration: 20–30 minutes.

## 2. Interview principles

- Start with the current security process, not the product.
- Focus on frequency, cost, bottlenecks, and trust.
- Avoid asking for sensitive vulnerabilities, credentials, or customer data.
- Ask about current testing practices and actual constraints.
- Distinguish between automated checkers and human-validated security review.

## 3. Interview flow

### Section A: Context and security posture

1. Can you tell me a bit about your company, your technical stack, and what you are building?
2. What systems or products are most exposed to risk today?
3. How does your team currently think about security testing?
4. Who is responsible for security decisions in the company?
5. What is the current maturity of your internal security processes?

### Section B: Current testing practices

6. How do you currently test your applications, APIs, or infrastructure for security issues?
7. How frequently do you perform security testing or review?
8. What kinds of issues are you most concerned about?
9. Do you use automated scanners, manual review, or external consultants?
10. Are there recurring bottlenecks in your current security process?

### Section C: Testing costs and providers

11. What do you currently spend on security testing, reviews, or consulting?
12. What providers or tools do you use today?
13. How do you evaluate whether a security review is worth paying for?
14. What are the biggest pain points with current vendors or testing providers?
15. Are there times when you postpone or avoid testing because of cost or speed?

### Section D: Release and product risk

16. How do you handle security reviews before releases or production changes?
17. What slows down release cycles or product launches today?
18. Are there situations where a vulnerability is discovered too late?
19. How do you decide which issues are urgent versus lower priority?
20. What is the impact of not catching issues early enough?

### Section E: Vulnerability management and validation

21. How do you triage findings once they are identified?
22. How do you decide whether a finding is real or a false positive?
23. How do you validate whether a vulnerability is exploitable in practice?
24. How do you track remediation and retesting?
25. Which security issues are hardest to validate and prioritise properly?

### Section F: Internal expertise and gaps

26. How much in-house security knowledge does the team have?
27. Where do you feel the biggest skills or capacity gaps are?
28. Are there technical teams that need stronger security support but do not have dedicated security staff?
29. What is the trade-off between speed and security quality in your team?
30. How do you decide when to bring in outside expertise?

### Section G: Reporting and remediation

31. What does a good security report need to include?
32. What is the most useful output from a security assessment for your team?
33. How do you communicate findings to engineering leadership or management?
34. What do you need to know before deciding whether to remediate an issue?
35. How do you track whether the issue was actually fixed?

### Section H: Trust in AI and human review

36. What has your experience been with AI tools in technical or security workflows?
37. What would make you trust an AI system to help with security testing?
38. What would make you uncomfortable with AI-led security work?
39. What level of human validation would be required before you trust the result?
40. Would you prefer an AI-assisted workflow, a human-led service, or a hybrid model?

### Section I: Buying decisions and budget

41. Who is involved in security spending or procurement decisions?
42. What would need to be true before you would buy a security-assessment product or service?
43. Would you prefer a subscription service, a project-based service, or a hybrid model?
44. Is there a realistic budget for security tooling or testing support?
45. What would be the strongest evidence that a security product is worth paying for?

## 4. Closing

46. Is there anything that you wish your current security processes did better?
47. Would it be reasonable to follow up with a second conversation if we wanted to explore this area further?

## 5. What to listen for

Strong signals include:

- repeated release-cycle bottlenecks,
- recurring security testing gaps,
- external consultant dependence,
- concern about false positives or weak validation,
- evidence of real spend on security tools or services,
- desire for faster triage and remediation support,
- willingness to discuss trust controls and human review.

Weak signals include:

- interest in AI without recurring security pain,
- no testing process in place,
- no spending and no urgency,
- no technical or security ownership,
- unwillingness to talk about process or tooling.

## 6. Red flags

Do not ask for:

- real credentials,
- production access,
- exploitation details,
- customer data,
- confidential vulnerabilities,
- internal security incidents that are not already authorised for discussion.

## 7. Interview best practices

- Keep the conversation technical, structured, and respectful.
- Avoid “security scare” language.
- Focus on operational realities and evidence-based pain.
- Capture direct quotes where relevant.
- Record whether the interviewee is a decision-maker, technical influencer, or channel partner.
- Do not convert polite interest into a buying signal.
