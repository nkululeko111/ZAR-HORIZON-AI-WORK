# Validation Decision Framework

## 1. Purpose

This framework defines the evidence required before making decisions about segment priority, product focus, pricing, and MVP scope. It does not replace founder approval.

The purpose is to keep the team disciplined: decisions should be driven by customer evidence, not optimism or product enthusiasm.

## 2. Decision categories

### Continue with the customer segment

Continue when there is evidence that:

- the problem is real and recurring,
- it is costly or risky enough to matter,
- a current workaround exists but is inadequate,
- the customer spends time or money on the issue,
- there is a plausible path to paying for a solution,
- the problem matches the approved product vision.

### Change the target segment

Change the target segment when:

- the same problem exists but the customer profile is wrong,
- the pain is real but the buyer is not the person who can pay,
- the market is too fragmented or too budget-constrained for early traction,
- the problem appears widespread but remains low urgency,
- interviews show a better-fit segment with stronger buying intent.

### Narrow the product's initial use cases

Narrow the initial use cases when:

- only a subset of the broader vision is consistently painful and actionable,
- there is evidence that some use cases are too vague or too expensive to solve early,
- a simpler starting workflow is far more likely to create value and revenue,
- the broader vision remains valid but should be deferred until the initial problem is proven.

### Reconsider pricing

Reconsider pricing when:

- customers show pain but not a clear willingness to pay,
- customers prefer project-based work or a low-cost model,
- the target problem is real but buyers expect pricing far below the current assumptions,
- the product is being compared to existing software or services with a different value perception,
- the current model does not match how customers already buy in the local market.

### Conduct more research

Conduct more research when:

- the sample is too small or too biased,
- some customer groups are unclear,
- evidence is conflicting across segments,
- the early conversations lack decision-maker access,
- the interviews show a mismatch between the problem and the proposed solution.

## 3. Evidence threshold for founder approval

A customer segment is strong enough to justify continued product focus when there is evidence of:

- recurring pain,
- clear frequency and severity,
- current spend or willingness to spend,
- dissatisfaction with current options,
- adequate fit with the approved product definition,
- realistic buying authority or access to a decision-maker.

A segment is weak when there is:

- limited pain,
- no budget path,
- low urgency,
- no evidence of spending or testing,
- generalised AI interest without operational or technical need.

## 4. Decision rules for founder review

The founder should review:

- the total number of interviews completed in each segment,
- the strongest recurring problem themes,
- genuine spend or budget signals,
- direct quotes describing pain and urgency,
- customer objections and trust concerns,
- frequency of buying intent and follow-up requests,
- whether the segment aligns with the approved product definition.

## 5. Recommended decision outcomes

### Business Partner

Prioritise segments where the problem is operationally painful, repeated weekly or monthly, and tied to time, cash flow, or missed commercial opportunities.

### AI Hacker

Prioritise segments where security testing is not optional, delivery speed matters, and the customer values both practical security validation and a clear path to remediation.

## 6. Not approved automatically

This framework should not be used to override approved product decisions without founder review. The task is to validate the market, not to silently change the product direction.

Any decision to:

- change the target segment,
- narrow the scope,
- adjust product positioning,
- change pricing assumptions,
- alter the use-case vision,

must be presented to the founder for approval.

## 7. Final recommendation

The product strategy should move forward only with evidence that the problem is real, costly, and well-understood, and that the customer sees enough value to pay for a solution that performs work within defined boundaries. Until that evidence exists, the roadmap should remain cautious and validation-led.
