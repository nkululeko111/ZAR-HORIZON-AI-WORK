## 2. Notes on evidence quality

- Some sources are official and directly relevant to South African policy, labour, and market context.
- Public pricing for local software and security products changes frequently and should be re-checked at purchase time.
- Market research on AI adoption and small-business software spend is often fragmented and not always directly comparable across segments.
- The current research environment does not provide guaranteed live access to every pricing page referenced by a vendor. Where that was the case, the research notes explicitly identify the limitation.

## 3. Source usage summary

- South Africa market overview references macroeconomic and policy sources to establish the environmental context.
- Customer-segment documents rely on business-behaviour reasoning, segment fit, and general market patterns rather than claims of exhaustive local survey statistics.
- Pricing and willingness-to-pay sections rely on general patterns and public software pricing structures, while clearly marking where exact price verification is incomplete.
- Final recommendations are framed as strategic priorities for validation, not as finalised commercial decisions.

## 4. Research limitation

This source register is intentionally conservative. It documents what was accessible and relevant, while clearly separating public facts from unsupported assumptions. Where exact South African pricing or segment-level adoption data was unavailable, the research notes identify the gap rather than filling it with speculation.
