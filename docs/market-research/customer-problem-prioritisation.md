# Customer Problem Prioritisation

## 1. Purpose

This document scores and ranks likely customer segments for each product using a weighted decision framework. The goal is to make sure we prioritise segments that have genuine pain, urgency, and credible willingness to pay rather than simply the largest market categories.

## 2. Scoring method

Each segment is scored from 1 to 5 against the following criteria:

- problem severity (15%)
- frequency of problem (15%)
- financial impact (15%)
- ability to pay (15%)
- willingness to adopt AI (10%)
- ease of reaching the customer (10%)
- competitive intensity (5%)
- recurring revenue potential (10%)
- fit with current product capabilities (5%)

Total possible score: 100

Interpretation:

- 80–100: strong initial priority
- 65–79: promising but needs focused validation
- 50–64: viable later-stage option
- below 50: weak initial focus

A large market size is not enough. A segment must also have demonstrable willingness and ability to pay for the solution.

### Interpretation

The AI Business Partner is strongest where the customer is already operating a business and has repeated pain around day-to-day commercial execution. The aspiring entrepreneur segment is strategically interesting but weak as a first revenue driver because affordability is usually limited and the customer may not yet be willing to pay for a business operating agent.

### Interpretation

The AI Hacker is strongest where the buyer has real security exposure, clear urgency, and either a direct technical function or a channel that can absorb recurring assessments. The most compelling category is not necessarily the largest enterprise customer; it is the externally exposed, commercially motivated buyer that needs speed, evidence, and practical remediation support.

IT service providers and MSPs score highly because they can package and resell security assessment service lines, creating a recurring revenue model. Established organisations are strategically important but harder to reach and harder to win with a new company.

## 6. Scoring rationale

The methodology intentionally gives more weight to:

- how painful the problem is,
- how often the problem repeats,
- whether the customer can pay,
- whether the problem is recurring enough to support subscription revenue,
- whether the product can realistically solve it with current capabilities.

This avoids overvaluing large but difficult-to-reach categories that look attractive on paper but are poor early entry markets for a new company.

## 7. Strategic conclusion

The strongest initial markets are not broad or abstract. They are specific customer situations with visible operational or security pain and a realistic path to purchase.

For the AI Business Partner, the best initial market is a mix of operational small businesses and founder-led SMEs that clearly need execution support.

For the AI Hacker, the strongest initial market is a mix of software builders, new SaaS businesses, and delivery partners such as MSPs that can package security assessments for clients.

## 8. Limitations

This scoring model is a directional market filter, not an approved pricing or sales plan. Public evidence on some local SME categories and cybersecurity budgets is incomplete. Actual customer interviews will be needed to validate willingness to pay and preferred buying behaviour.
