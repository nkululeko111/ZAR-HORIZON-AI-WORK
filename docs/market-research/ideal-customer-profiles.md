# Ideal Customer Profiles

## 1. Research purpose

This document identifies the strongest initial customer profiles for each product. These are not the biggest possible companies. They are the most practical early opportunities for a new South African technology company with limited capital and a need to prove outcomes quickly.

## 2. AI Business Partner — recommended ICPs

### ICP 1: Owner-managed service or retail business with recurring customer demand

- Industry: retail, hospitality, local services, food, trade
- Business size: 1–20 employees
- Geographic focus: Johannesburg, Cape Town, Durban, local township and suburban commercial clusters
- Business maturity: early-stage operational business with recurring sales but limited operational systems
- Decision-maker: owner or founder
- Main problems: cash-flow visibility, weak stock control, manual admin, inconsistent follow-up, weak reporting
- Buying triggers: revenue pressure, rising admin burden, unreliable records, desire to improve profitability without hiring staff
- Current alternatives: accounting software, spreadsheets, POS systems, basic bookkeeping support, local admin services
- Expected product usage: daily monitoring of sales, expenses, lead follow-up, quote generation, reminder workflows
- Likely purchasing objections: cost sensitivity, scepticism about AI quality, concern about data quality and trust
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: direct help with business operations, not generic advice; clear ROI language; practical execution and workflow support

### ICP 2: Established small business with sales and customer management pain

- Industry: professional services, business-to-business services, construction support, home services, agency work, logistics support
- Business size: 5–50 employees
- Geographic focus: urban and peri-urban business hubs
- Business maturity: operationally stable, recurring customer base, fragmented admin processes
- Decision-maker: managing director, operations lead, founder owner
- Main problems: poor business reporting, weak pipeline visibility, missed follow-up, slow admin, inconsistent supplier and customer data
- Buying triggers: revenue leakage, missed sales opportunities, demand for better reporting
- Current alternatives: CRM tools, accounting platforms, outsourced admin support, spreadsheets, consultants
- Expected product usage: weekly reporting, lead tracking, customer follow-up, proposal drafting, task management
- Likely purchasing objections: integration friction, risk of wrong automation, value perception
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: improved operational visibility and execution support without needing an internal analyst or junior operations team

### ICP 3: Growing SME needing stronger decision support and workflow automation

- Industry: professional services, media, technology-enabled services, distribution, health services, education support
- Business size: 10–100 employees
- Geographic focus: Johannesburg and Cape Town 
- Business maturity: growing, formalising, under pressure to scale without adding overhead
- Decision-maker: founder, CEO, operations manager
- Main problems: weak decision data, manual reporting, inefficient lead generation, poor performance monitoring, fragmented supplier information
- Buying triggers: growth stage, need for tighter operational control, cash management pressure, scaling bottlenecks
- Current alternatives: business intelligence tools, consultants, spreadsheet-heavy reporting, CRM systems
- Expected product usage: performance dashboards, opportunity tracking, supplier and customer intelligence, operational summaries, recurring reporting
- Likely purchasing objections: data quality, integration complexity, need for human oversight
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: practical commercial intelligence and execution support aligned to recurring growth and workflow needs

## 3. AI Hacker — recommended ICPs

### ICP 1: Software company or application builder with strong release velocity

- Industry: software development, SaaS, digital products, e-commerce platforms, API businesses
- Business size: 5–200 employees
- Geographic focus: Johannesburg, Cape Town, Pretoria, tech hubs and digital service clusters
- Business maturity: product or service company with active releases and customer-facing digital systems
- Decision-maker: CTO, engineering manager, product lead, founder
- Main problems: limited security review capacity, repeated application testing bottlenecks, need for validation before release, exposure of customer-facing services
- Buying triggers: product launches, new platform features, customer trust concerns, investor or compliance pressure
- Current alternatives: third-party penetration testing, software consultants, generic scanners, internal engineering review
- Expected product usage: periodic app and API security review, exploitability validation, vulnerability triage, remediation guidance
- Likely purchasing objections: trust in AI-generated findings, need for human validation, legal and risk concerns
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: faster, evidence-backed assessment workflow that complements existing engineering and external security review

### ICP 2: Startup or SaaS business needing affordable security validation

- Industry: fintech, SaaS, B2B software, digital commerce, data-driven services
- Business size: 3–50 employees
- Geographic focus: digital-first startup environments, early market-facing technology businesses
- Business maturity: early-stage or growth-stage business with digital infrastructure but limited security staffing
- Decision-maker: founder, CTO, product owner
- Main problems: limited budget, security gaps, weak security testing process, need to build customer trust
- Buying triggers: launch, funding events, enterprise customer demand, product maturity milestones
- Current alternatives: occasional external tests, basic scanning tools, ad hoc engineering review
- Expected product usage: recurring checks around release cycles, attack-surface review, remediation support, retesting
- Likely purchasing objections: affordability, need to see clear evidence, concern that AI is not trusted for genuine security validation
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: practical security assessment support at a lower cost and with structured evidence that helps teams prioritise remediation

### ICP 3: MSP or IT services provider with security service expansion goals

- Industry: managed IT, managed services, infrastructure support, cybersecurity advisory
- Business size: 10–200 employees
- Geographic focus: urban IT service markets and partner networks
- Business maturity: established service provider with existing client relationships and recurring deliverables
- Decision-maker: partner, general manager, security service lead
- Main problems: limited internal security capacity, need for annual or recurring assessment workflows, pressure to expand service value
- Buying triggers: client security requirements, growth ambitions, need to create recurring revenue lines
- Current alternatives: external pentesting vendors, scanners, manual security review engagements, security advisory retainers
- Expected product usage: structured recurring assessments, white-label evaluation workflows, evidence-driven reporting, remediation guidance
- Likely purchasing objections: service quality control, risk of over-automation, need for human validation and accountability
- Reasons they would choose ZAR HORIZON: a repeatable security assessment capability that can be packaged into client service offerings and supported by human review

## 4. Most commercially promising initial profiles

### AI Business Partner

The strongest entry profile is:

- owner-managed small retail or service business with recurring sales and operational pressure,
- focused on profitability, supplier and customer management, and workflow execution,
- willing to pay for practical help that reduces admin and improves visibility.

### AI Hacker

The strongest entry profile is:

- a software or SaaS company with active customer-facing digital systems,
- limited internal security capacity,
- clear release and trust concerns,
- need for repeatable, evidence-backed security validation without a heavy enterprise procurement process.

## 5. Research note

These profiles are strategic recommendations for initial product focus. They are not approved pricing decisions or final go-to-market plans. Actual customer validation is still required before finalising a market-entry strategy.
