# Pricing and Willingness to Pay

## 1. Research note

This document is intentionally cautious. The current research environment does not provide reliable live access to all relevant South African pricing pages, and many local product prices shift frequently by plan, VAT, and billing cycle. Public pricing is therefore treated as a market signal, not as a final approved pricing model.

The purpose of this document is to identify:

- where public South African pricing evidence exists,
- which market segments appear willing to pay,
- which pricing patterns are realistic for the two product lines,
- what still needs validation with actual customers.

## 2. Business Partner pricing context

### Relevant market patterns

The Business Partner sits in a category of operational software, business intelligence, workflow automation, and admin support. In South Africa, businesses already pay recurring monthly fees for software that reduces paperwork, improves reporting, and supports customer and supplier management.

Examples of categories and providers with strong local visibility include:

- accounting software,
- CRM systems,
- bookkeeping platforms,
- workflow automation tools,
- business management software.

### Market signal

The strongest willingness-to-pay is typically found in businesses that:

- already use accounting or CRM tools,
- have recurring customer volumes,
- have cash-flow pressure,
- have specialist operational bottlenecks,
- can see a direct productivity or revenue gain from an automated subscription.

### Pricing observations

Public South African pricing information is often published in ZAR for software such as accounting and operational tools. However, these prices typically vary by:

- plan level,
- number of users,
- automation volume,
- reporting features,
- VAT treatment,
- annual vs monthly billing.

This means exact local pricing should not be treated as fixed without live validation. The practical market signal is that small and medium businesses are comfortable paying recurring fees where the software clearly helps them manage cash flow, customers, invoices, and operations.

### Preliminary pricing hypothesis

For the AI Business Partner, the most realistic market entry is likely to be a low-to-mid subscription tier priced for small businesses or SMEs that already pay for accounting or CRM software. The product should be positioned around operational value and repeatable task execution rather than broad “AI assistant” language alone.

A plausible pricing direction, pending customer validation, is:

- starter package for micro and small businesses: low monthly subscription at a price point that is meaningful but lower than a full outsourced admin function,
- growth package for established SMEs: higher recurring fee tied to reporting, workflow support, and execution depth,
- professional package for service businesses and growing SMEs: premium subscription for higher-volume support and advisory workflows.

These are hypotheses only and not approved pricing decisions.

## 3. AI Hacker pricing context

### Relevant market patterns

The AI Hacker sits in a category that blends automated security testing with external professional security assessment. In South Africa, customers often pay for:

- vulnerability assessments,
- penetration testing,
- managed security services,
- security monitoring subscriptions,
- technical assurance and compliance reviews.

### Market signal

The strongest willingness to pay is highest where the customer has:

- digital exposure,
- customer or investor trust obligations,
- active product development or public-facing systems,
- limited internal security capability,
- a direct risk of business disruption if a vulnerability goes unresolved.

### Pricing observations

Public pricing for professional security services in South Africa can vary significantly. Penetration testing and security review work is typically priced by:

- project scope,
- number of assets,
- testing depth,
- assumptions about red-team engagement,
- retesting hours,
- human reporting and remediation support.

Many local and regional security providers publish service packages rather than one fixed price. Automated security platforms often use recurring SaaS pricing, but this is less transparent for bespoke security review engagements.

### Preliminary pricing hypothesis

For the AI Hacker, the most realistic initial commercial angle is not a broad “unlimited offensive security agent” model. It is a controlled assessment service with structured deliverables, evidence management, and human review. This suggests a mix of:

- a lower recurring subscription for continuous or periodic assessment assistance,
- a project-based package for targeted app or infrastructure review,
- a higher-touch retest and remediation support layer.

This would fit customers who want faster security validation but still require professional trust and evidence. Such a model is more realistic than assuming broad enterprise red-team budgets for early entry.

## 4. Published price evidence and gaps

### Evidence available

Examples of public price-carrying sources in the South African market included:

- Xero South Africa pricing pages
- Zoho Books pricing pages
- local and regional accounting and business software pricing pages
- public security-service provider pages and product pricing pages

These sources show that local customers are accustomed to recurring software fees and that business tools are priced in ZAR with local billing assumptions.

### Evidence gaps

The following items were not consistently confirmable in the current environment and require validation with live pricing checks or direct customer interviews:

- exact current ZAR pricing for all major competitors in each segment,
- exact monthly subscription pricing for local AI business-assistant offerings,
- exact South African local pricing for managed security and penetration testing packages,
- current pricing of AI-assisted security testing vendors in the region,
- VAT and billing-cycle differences by provider and plan.

This is a material limitation and should be treated as a real research gap, not a sign that prices are absent.

## 5. Willingness to pay by product

### AI Business Partner

Strongest willingness to pay in:

- businesses with recurring revenue,
- established operations with existing accounting or admin tools,
- businesses suffering from lead leakage, stock issues, operational delays, and weak reporting.

Lower willingness to pay in:

- pre-launch founders,
- highly informal micro-businesses with minimal digitisation,
- segments that prefer free tools or informal support.

### AI Hacker

Strongest willingness to pay in:

- digital product and SaaS companies,
- organisations with customer-facing apps or cloud infrastructure,
- MSPs and IT service providers that can bundle the service into recurring client revenue,
- businesses that need stronger assurance without full enterprise red-team budgets.

Lower willingness to pay in:

- low-security-maturity SMEs that do not yet see a direct risk reduction case,
- organisations that require a legacy enterprise procurement process before buying security services.

## 6. Strategic implication

The main pricing lesson is that customers will pay for these products when they can clearly connect them to:

- revenue protection,
- reduced admin burden,
- lower risk,
- better compliance or customer trust,
- faster security validation,
- recurring service value.

The pricing model should not assume that “AI” alone creates demand. The product must prove operational or security utility in language the customer understands.

## 7. Recommendation for Day 3 validation

Customer validation should focus on:

- what they currently pay for accounting, CRM, or security tools,
- what the cost of delay or failure is,
- how they currently buy operational or security services,
- whether they would prefer subscription or project-based engagements,
- which workflows are most expensive to do manually or poorly.

This is the highest-value next research step before any pricing approval.
