# Research methodology and sources

## Objective

This research was designed to understand who already solves the business and cybersecurity problems described in the product definitions, how those systems operate, how they are priced, and where customer pain remains underserved.

The task is to document competitive reality, not to create marketing claims or declare product superiority without evidence.

## Methodology

1. Reviewed the approved ZAR HORIZON product definitions, customer segments and use-case frameworks to maintain product alignment.
2. Prioritised South African and African market context, while adding internationally relevant benchmark products.
3. Used official company websites, product documentation, pricing pages, and public product materials as the primary evidence base.
4. Recorded whether each fact was:
   - Verified
   - Vendor-reported
   - Third-party reported
   - Estimated
   - Unavailable
   - Unverified
5. Kept direct product capability claims separate from inferred competitive advantages.
6. Avoided assuming pricing, distribution or market share where public data was not available.

## Research categories

### AI Business Partner research

- AI business agents and digital business assistants

### AI Hacker research

## Research quality notes

- Several local South African security consultancies are clearly active in the market, but many do not publish open pricing or detailed AI-agent capabilities in a way that supports a direct comparison.
- Public AI and security platform marketing often emphasises automation and validation, but not all vendor claims can be independently verified in the same way as product features or published pricing.
- For AI-Hacker, product capability claims need to be interpreted carefully: real commercial value often depends on scope control, evidence quality, human oversight, and retesting discipline rather than the mere fact that an AI layer is present.
- For Business Partner, pricing is often segmented by users, modules and integrations; therefore, public plan pricing should be read as a pricing benchmark rather than a final benchmark for all user types.

## Evidence treatment

Examples of evidence treatment in this research:

- Verified: Sage South Africa homepage states, “Try free for 30 days, then from R270/month.”
- Verified: Zoho Books South Africa pricing page publishes annualised pricing for Standard, Professional, Premium, Elite and Ultimate plans.
- Verified: Invicti advertises Agentic Pentest pricing at “$500 max per pentest.”
- Verified: Pentera states it validates real exploitability and risk reduction.
- Unavailable / quote-based: many enterprise security vendors do not publish direct public pricing.
- Unverified: specific “AI autonomy” claims must be treated with caution unless a vendor offers reproducible proof, scope controls and reporting standards.

## Research limitations

- Public pricing does not always reflect local VAT treatment, local taxes, implementation costs or multi-user add-ons.
- The market changes continuously; this research reflects the findings visible at the time of investigation.
- Local African pricing and implementation realities often require direct sales conversations; therefore, price tables should be treated as directional markers rather than final commercial benchmarks.

## Conclusion

The most reliable competitive research comes from official sources that describe product scope, feature set, delivery model and public pricing. Where public information is absent, the correct research outcome is to label it as unavailable, quote-based, or unverified, and avoid converting that uncertainty into a firm claim.
