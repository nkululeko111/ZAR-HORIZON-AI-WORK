# Day 6 competitive research decisions

## Purpose

This document captures the decisions, open questions and founder review items that arise from the competitive and alternative-solution research.

## Findings requiring founder approval

### Business Partner decisions

1. Whether the product should aim first at accounting and finance workflows, sales workflow automation, or a broader full-lifecycle business partner.
2. Whether the initial launch should target a narrower SME segment, such as service businesses or retail/inventory-led businesses, or attempt a broader multi-industry range.
3. Whether the product should lean into modular SaaS integration, a single-platform experience, or a lighter workflow-focused assistant model.
4. Whether South African SME context should be treated as a strategic differentiator in the initial market-entry plan.
5. Whether the product should emphasise “execution with evidence” as a core positioning message in GTM materials.

### AI Hacker decisions

1. Whether the first commercial focus should be on web app and API testing, infrastructure review, or cloud/security configuration coverage.
2. Whether the market entry should target software companies, SaaS operators, MSPs, or a mix of digital businesses and consultative security buyers.
3. Whether the product should emphasise enterprise-grade validation features or a more accessible mid-market product architecture.
4. Whether the product should position itself as a managed service, a SaaS platform, or a hybrid model with human oversight.
5. Whether “AI red-team” positioning should be used in public messaging, or whether a more evidence-first and trust-led positioning is preferable.

## Research gaps and customer validation needs

### Business Partner

- Local SMB appetite for integrated business planning and execution must be validated with customers.
- The best market-entry segment needs real interviews and a test of willingness to adopt a business-operating agent.
- The value of cross-system workflow automation versus point-solution efficiency must be confirmed.

### AI Hacker

- The market likely wants proof and governance more than sheer autonomy; this should be validated with customer buyers.
- The right entry segment may be software teams and digital businesses rather than broad enterprise security.
- The level of human oversight customers expect must be confirmed before marketing the product as autonomous.

## Strategic decisions already supported by evidence

- The market is real and active for both segments.
- The strongest competitors are not necessarily direct replacements for these products.
- There is meaningful opportunity in the “research-to-execution” layer for the Business Partner.
- There is meaningful opportunity in the “authorised, evidence-first, retestable” layer for the AI Hacker.
- The strongest product positioning should avoid overselling AI as generic magic and instead focus on observable execution outcomes, safe controls and clear evidence.

## Decisions that should not be treated as final product commitments

- exact pricing strategy;
- broad self-serve MVP scope;
- launch segment commitment without customer validation;
- direct competitor superiority claims;
- final delivery model choice between SaaS, consulting, managed service or hybrid model.

## Final research conclusion

The competitor landscape shows that both product categories are real, but also fragmented. That means ZAR HORIZON should not try to imitate broad category leaders in every feature area. Instead, it should identify the best seat in the market where evidence, execution and trust matter most.

This is a valid strategic direction, but final product-market positioning still requires founder review and customer validation.
