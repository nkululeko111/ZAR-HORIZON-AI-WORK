# AI Hacker pricing benchmark

## Objective

This benchmark summarises the public pricing patterns in the AI Hacker market, including both commercial security platforms and open-source alternatives. Public pricing in this market is often incomplete because many security products are sold through quoting, project scope and implementation services.

## Public pricing observations

### Invicti

- Invicti’s public pricing page states: “Agentic Pentest – $500 max per pentest.”
- The broader platform is structured as package-based product pricing, with multiple AppSec options rather than a single flat fee.
- Source status: Verified vendor-reported pricing for the agentic pentest product line.

### Pentera

### XBOW

### South African and regional service providers

- Local and regional security firms typically operate as project-based providers, not self-service software subscriptions.
- Source status: pricing is generally quote-based and not publicly standardised.

## Pricing patterns in the market

### 1. Commercial AI security products are usually quote-based

The most established AI-driven security platforms do not typically expose a single standardised monthly price in public material.

### 2. Security product pricing is scope-driven

Price depends on variables such as:

- number of assets;
- applications or APIs in scope;
- testing method;
- environment type;
- required human review and remediation support;
- deployment model and compliance requirements.

### 3. Open-source tools remain strong for technical teams

Open-source tools can be assembled into a capable testing environment, but they generally require more operator expertise, setup and governance than a commercial platform.

### 4. Local providers often sell projects, not subscriptions

This is important because the local South African market may prefer consulting-led delivery for security assurance, especially where compliance or expert validation is required.

## Commercial interpretation

The AI Hacker market is not a flat SaaS landscape. It is best understood as a mix of:

- low-cost or free tooling for practitioners;
- project-based consulting and pentest services;
- enterprise attack-surface validation platforms;
- AI-assisted or autonomous security agents with custom pricing.

This means a future ZAR HORIZON deployment model may need to balance between self-service, project-based and managed-service offerings rather than assuming one uniform commercial model.

## Conclusion

The recurring pattern is that pricing is either:

- open-source / low-cost for do-it-yourself practitioners;
- quote-based / enterprise for automated or AI validation platforms;
- project-based for local consultancy-style pentesting.

This supports a potential delivery strategy for ZAR HORIZON AI @WORK that could occupy a middle ground, but only if the product demonstrates strong safety controls, evidence quality and scope discipline.
