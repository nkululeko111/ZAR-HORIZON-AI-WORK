# Consolidated founder decision register — Days 1–14

## How to use this register

Recommendations are not decisions. The founder or designated product/security owner must record the chosen option and date before an item is treated as approved. “Blocks MVP” refers to the relevant product capability, not low-risk repository setup or discovery work.

## A. Must be resolved before committing to product development

| ID | Area | Decision required / why it matters | Options | Recommendation and trade-offs | If postponed | Blocks MVP? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FDR-01 | Product strategy | Select first product and the first end-to-end workflow; determines architecture, scope, and team focus | Business Partner performance workflow; Hacker controlled assessment; discovery only | Validate Business Partner first, then choose a narrow workflow. Lower execution risk, but demand is unvalidated; Hacker could be preferred if interviews support it | Parallel scope and premature implementation | Yes, for product feature commitment |
| FDR-02 | Customer validation | Confirm initial buyer, segment, problem urgency, existing workaround, and willingness to pay | Run discovery interviews; use existing assumptions; defer commercial build | Complete structured interviews for both products; record evidence, not just target profiles | False demand and pricing assumptions | Yes, for committing product scope |
| FDR-03 | MVP scope | Approve or reject the Day 7 recommended scope, exclusions, outcome, and acceptance criteria | Approve a narrow slice; revise it; continue discovery | Approve one measurable vertical workflow with explicit exclusions | No stable implementation backlog or finish line | Yes |
| FDR-04 | Decision authority | Establish which documents/decisions are founder-approved versus proposals; current wording can imply all design docs are approved | Mark approved decisions explicitly; treat all docs as advisory; approve by phase | Maintain explicit decision status in registers; preserve vision while avoiding implied approval of proposals | Conflicting interpretation and unauthorized scope | Yes, for spec-driven implementation |
| FDR-05 | Product success | Approve initial success measures for the chosen workflow | Define workflow-specific outcome metrics; use general usage metrics; defer | Define measurable outcome and quality/safety measures before pilot | Cannot assess whether MVP works or should continue | Yes, for MVP acceptance |

## B. Must be resolved before the relevant feature is implemented

| ID | Area | Decision required / why it matters | Options | Recommendation and trade-offs | If postponed | Blocks MVP? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FDR-06 | Identity and access | Define identity provider, organisation membership, workspace roles, service identities, and privileged access | Simple app roles; managed identity provider plus app roles; staged approach | Decide before authenticated tenant data; staged approach limits early scope but needs clear authorization contracts | Insecure or inconsistent access behavior | Yes, before user/tenant data |
| FDR-07 | Tenant enforcement | Choose application-layer scoped queries alone or layered enforcement with PostgreSQL RLS | Application enforcement; app + RLS; other reviewed design | Decide after threat-model review; layered controls can reduce blast radius but add operational complexity | Incomplete isolation design and rework | Yes, before tenant-data features |
| FDR-08 | Product permissions | Define product entitlements and non-inheriting capability/role matrix | Separate product grants; shared role inheritance; staged grants | Separate explicit product grants; strongest separation, more policy work | Cross-product privilege leakage | Yes, before either product’s privileged capability |
| FDR-09 | Approval policy | Choose approvers, delegation, thresholds, expiry, cancellation, exact action binding, and reapproval rules | Per-action manual approval; role-delegated approval; risk-tiered policy | Risk-tiered policy with exact action/parameter binding and explicit delegation; more process, better control | Approval bypass, ambiguous responsibility, replay risk | Yes, before external or high-risk actions |
| FDR-10 | Audit and evidence | Set audit/evidence ownership, access, integrity, retention, deletion, and user export policy | Product-specific retention; shared policy; configurable retention | Define a minimum auditable lifecycle before storing sensitive evidence; flexibility costs implementation effort | Privacy, compliance, dispute, and storage risks | Yes, before evidence-bearing feature |
| FDR-11 | Business Partner data | Select initial data source/import format, supported records, validation, period/currency assumptions, and VAT treatment | Manual structured input; file import; one approved integration | Start with one bounded source or controlled import and deterministic validation; limited coverage but easier to verify | Unbounded ingestion scope and unreliable calculations | Yes, for financial workflow |
| FDR-12 | Business Partner advice | Define financial recommendation limits, confidence/source presentation, escalation, and professional disclaimer | Informational only; human-reviewed operational recommendations; broader advice | Keep outputs informational/operational and explicitly not a substitute for regulated professional advice | Misleading or overreaching financial guidance | Yes, for financial recommendations |
| FDR-13 | Business Partner actions | Select which external actions, if any, are in the first MVP and define duplicate prevention/verification | No external action; one approval-gated action; multiple integrations | Begin read-only or with one approval-gated action after idempotency and verification are designed | Uncontrolled side effects or scope growth | Blocks action execution, not analysis |
| FDR-14 | AI Hacker authorization | Define proof of authority, asset ownership/permission verification, approved scope, exclusions, time window, and revocation | Customer attestation; verified authorization artifacts; human review combination | Require explicit recorded authorization and review; slower onboarding but commercially trustworthy | Legal and safety exposure | Yes, before any assessment |
| FDR-15 | AI Hacker execution | Approve worker isolation, network egress, credentials, resource limits, tool levels, and emergency stop | No automated execution; isolated allowlisted worker; broader controlled environment | Start without autonomous execution; design and test a deny-by-default isolated worker before enabling tools | Unsafe execution or unbounded blast radius | Yes, before automated assessment |
| FDR-16 | Findings and evidence | Define severity/confidence rubric, false-positive review, evidence integrity, remediation and retest criteria | Human-reviewed findings; rules-based rubric; staged combination | Human-reviewed findings with explicit evidence/confidence and retest record; more labor, higher trust | Unreliable reports and unverifiable claims | Yes, before commercial reports |
| FDR-17 | API contracts | Approve auth context, errors, idempotency, async states, cancellation, and versioning for first workflow | Implement current catalog; revise contract first; staged API | Freeze only the selected workflow contract after security decisions; avoids implementing broad speculative APIs | Contract churn and client mismatch | Yes, for API implementation |
| FDR-18 | UX and accessibility | Approve visual direction, approval delegation, automation level, and states for incomplete/uncertain data | Use existing wireframes; revise after user research; minimal pilot UI | Validate the chosen journey with target users before final UI; delays polish but avoids wrong workflows | UX/contract mismatch | Blocks customer-ready UI, not engineering setup |
| FDR-19 | AI providers and integrations | Select provider/model, data-use terms, fallback, and permitted integrations | Defer; one provider; abstraction with multiple providers | Defer until a product slice requires it; one provider may simplify MVP but creates dependency | Unclear cost/data controls or unnecessary abstraction | Blocks provider-dependent features |
| FDR-20 | Billing and commercial model | Decide usage/billing approach and limits for paid pilot | Manual pilot billing; subscription; usage-based; defer | Defer full billing until value and usage are validated; manual pilot needs operational effort | Commercial launch cannot be automated | No for technical MVP; yes for scaled paid launch |

## C. Can be postponed until later, but before production or scale

| ID | Area | Decision required / why it matters | Options | Recommendation and trade-offs | If postponed | Blocks MVP? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FDR-21 | Hosting and region | Choose cloud provider, deployment region, and data-residency posture | South African region if suitable; other regional/global cloud; compare providers | Compare cost, availability, residency, managed services, and operational capability before production; keeps options open now | Production deployment cannot be finalized | No for local MVP; yes for production |
| FDR-22 | Production database | Choose managed PostgreSQL or self-managed service and recovery characteristics | Managed database; self-hosted; defer production choice | Prefer managed PostgreSQL for early production if cost and region fit; less ops burden, provider dependency | No production data platform | No for local MVP |
| FDR-23 | Secrets management | Select production secret manager, rotation, and access policy | Cloud-native manager; external vault; restricted interim process | Use a managed solution once hosting is chosen; simplifies lifecycle but ties operations to platform | No safe production secret supply | No for local MVP; yes for production |
| FDR-24 | Retention and deletion | Set data/evidence retention periods, customer deletion, and legal-hold policy | Fixed product policy; configurable by data class; legal review | Decide per data class with legal/privacy input; more nuanced but safer | Storage and privacy obligations unclear | No for prototype; yes before production data |
| FDR-25 | Backup RPO/RTO | Set retention, RPO, RTO, recovery test frequency, and ownership | Low-cost best effort; tiered objectives; stricter commitments | Choose tiered targets after pilot and cost assessment; balances cost and recovery needs | Recovery commitments and design remain unknown | No for local MVP; yes before production |
| FDR-26 | Incident communications | Assign incident commander, customer/regulatory notification authority, timelines, and support responsibility | Founder-led; named operations/security owner; contracted support | Name an accountable owner before production; operational burden but necessary accountability | Delayed or inconsistent response | No for local MVP; yes before production |
| FDR-27 | Monitoring and cost | Set alert coverage, response hours, provider/model cost limits, and budget | Basic business-hours alerts; managed monitoring; 24/7 service | Start with proportionate alerts and explicit cost budgets; less expense, narrower coverage | Undetected failures or uncontrolled cost | No for local MVP; yes before production/pilot |
| FDR-28 | Independent security review | Decide review scope, timing, and independence for tenant/auth and Hacker execution controls | Internal review; external review; both at risk gates | Require independent review before enabling high-risk Hacker execution and before production handling of sensitive data; cost and lead time | Blind spots remain in high-impact controls | No for local MVP; yes before high-risk launch |
| FDR-29 | Scale and performance | Approve measurable latency, concurrency, execution duration, and resource targets | Set after pilot; define early targets; tier by workflow | Measure pilot workload first and set targets before scaling; avoids invented SLAs | Cannot make production capacity commitments | No for initial local slice |
| FDR-30 | Market expansion and branding | Confirm expansion regions, final brand direction, and localization priorities | South Africa first; broader African markets; staged expansion | Validate South Africa-first assumptions before expansion; focus reduces scope | Broader market plans remain uncertain | No for technical MVP |
