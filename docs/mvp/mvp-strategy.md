# MVP strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the initial MVP strategy for the two founder-approved specialist products in ZAR HORIZON AI @WORK:

- AI Business Partner
- AI Hacker

The goal is to identify the smallest commercially useful product releases that still demonstrate real execution value, preserve the approved product vision, and avoid premature expansion into unvalidated product scope.

## Core MVP principles

1. The MVP must solve a real, repeated customer problem.
2. The workflow must be complete end-to-end, not a collection of isolated features.
3. The product must still behave as an execution-oriented specialist agent, not a passive chatbot or static dashboard.
4. The first release should be constrained but credible.
5. The product must separate execution, evidence, and approval boundaries clearly.
6. The MVP must be limited to evidence-backed, founder-compatible scope.

## Evaluation framework

Each proposed feature is scored from 1 to 5 on the following criteria:

1. Customer problem severity
2. Customer value
3. Frequency of use
4. Commercial potential
5. Competitive differentiation
6. Technical feasibility
7. Development complexity
8. Integration dependency
9. Data availability
10. Security, legal and operational risk

### Scoring method

- 1 = weak or absent
- 2 = limited value or high friction
- 3 = moderate importance
- 4 = strong value or fit
- 5 = very strong value, product-critical, or highly differentiated

### Interpretation

The score is not a final decision rule. High scores are useful but insufficient on their own because a feature may be valuable yet impossible to deliver cleanly in the first release. Conversely, a feature may be less glamorous but essential to trust, safety, or product viability.

Important trade-offs include:

- value vs implementation difficulty;
- narrow usefulness vs strong product fit;
- customer demand vs integration dependency;
- differentiation vs risk;
- operational utility vs legal/security exposure.

### Preliminary status

All scores in the MVP work are preliminary and should be treated as product design assessments rather than validated customer evidence.

## Proposed MVP screening logic

A feature should generally be considered for the MVP only when it checks several of the following conditions:

- directly supports a core user workflow;
- is used repeatedly in real business or security work;
- requires proof, execution and reporting rather than one-off advice;
- can be delivered with a realistic amount of integration and data assumptions;
- can be verified with evidence and customer review;
- does not create unacceptable legal, operational or security risk in the first release.

## MVP boundary rule

Features that require heavy custom integration, specialist human validation for every run, or broad regulatory/ethical review should not be treated as MVP foundations unless they are essential to the core promise of the product.

## Product-specific focus

### AI Business Partner

The first release should focus on a complete workflow that helps a business owner or operator:

- understand the business context;
- evaluate key business signals;
- decide on an operationally useful action;
- generate a business document or task;
- execute an approved action within a controlled workflow;
- report and verify the result.

### AI Hacker

The first release should focus on a complete workflow that helps an organisation:

- register authorised assets;
- define scope and exclusions;
- conduct a bounded security assessment;
- validate findings with evidence;
- explain risk and remediation;
- verify the fix after remediation.

## Recommended product sequencing

1. Define and validate the primary workflow for each product.
2. Deliver the smallest product that demonstrates the workflow end-to-end.
3. Prioritise trusted execution, evidence capture and safe approval models.
4. Expand only after the initial workflow proves operational value.

## Conclusion

The MVP should not be a feature list; it should be a narrow but complete operating workflow. The product must be useful enough that a customer sees real value in the workflow, and safe enough that the platform can support it without unchecked risk.
